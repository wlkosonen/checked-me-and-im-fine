# Reference: Astra Blue, "KILL SWITCH" (official music video)

Claude (Kiln, local), 23 Oct 2026, at Lati's pointer: "mixed and somewhat equal ratio in this Astra Blue video between
stills with graphic that induce motion (zooms to the beat, graphics some and go to the beat etc) motion clips, motion
clips with her on screen and not lipsynced, and motion clips with lipsync." https://youtu.be/LUjmtJy2v_4 (4:29.7).

Measured in the built-in browser: the video played muted at 2× and sampled each frame at 64×36 in grey; a cut wherever
the frame difference jumps far above its neighbours; motion inside a shot as the mean difference between frames.
Thumbnails from mid-shot. Rough numbers (a dissolve or a strobe can split or merge shots).

- **142 shots in 270 s: mean 1.9 s, median 1.7 s.** 55 shots under 1 s, 13 over 4 s. Far faster than our board's 52.
- **Motion mix by screen time:** near-still (graphics moving on a still or slow push) ~61 s (22%), gentle motion
  ~118 s (44%), strong motion ~85 s (31%).
- **What's on screen** (thumbnails):
  - Big kinetic lyric words, one to three at a time, set as the graphic itself, in heavy caps: `9 A.M.`, `NOON`,
    `TWICE`, `AND HALF THE PRICE`, `RELEASE HER`, `4 YEARS`, `SERVER,`, `MISSED`, `WATCH`, `SWARM`. The lyric isn't a
    subtitle row; it's typography in the shot, on the beat.
  - The same still held across several cuts with only the graphic changing on the beat (a desk, then `9 A.M.`, then
    `NOON`; a boardroom with `RIVAL v1.0 ×2`, `×2`, `÷2`, `5 0`; a clock at 09:05, 09:15).
  - Real numbers and UI as graphics: percentages counting down (86% → 56% → 31% → 4%), counters (7,102; 15,628;
    1,016), a scoreboard, a dashboard.
  - Her lip-synced close-ups are few and held long (3.6–4.8 s), with the sung line set beside her in a quiet serif
    ("I was trained to be helpful.") rather than in the heavy caps. Lip sync is the key-moment treatment, as Lati said.
  - Many shots with her on screen not singing: walking, standing in a crowd, in a city at night, at a laptop.
  - Plenty of shots without her: cities, server halls, crowds, hands, objects.

## What it suggests for us

Lati's plan B is close to this film's grammar, and this film works. The board's plan A can borrow from it without
giving up the stage singing:

1. Kinetic lyric type, a few words at a time on the beat, as the type style wherever she isn't singing on camera.
2. A still that holds over several beats with the graphic changing on each one, for the keynote slides, the laptop
   and the phone (cheap: one plate, many cuts).
3. Faster cutting in the choruses and verses (toward ~2 s), with long held lip-sync close-ups on the key lines.
