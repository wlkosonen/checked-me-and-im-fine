# Plan B: reduced scope

Claude (Kiln, local), 23 Oct 2026. Lati: "Lets have a plan b for reduced scope. Singing and lip sync for key moments
like a few lines on stage, per stage part while performing, and when looking at the camera directly in other shots.
Rest is is graphics/motion/ etc. Lyrics video paired with important shots. … But the goal is plan A."

Plan A is `board.json` (52 shots, 45 of them sung). Plan B keeps the same 52 shots, the same cuts, chapters, devices and
graphics, so we can drop to it shot by shot at any point without re-boarding. What changes is who sings on camera.

## The rule

- **Lip sync stays on 16 shots:** the mic check, one line per keynote/desk/Q&A chapter sung straight into the lens, two
  or three phrases per chorus on stage, the bridge's first and last lines, and the final "Hi". These are the lines that
  carry the film.
- **Everywhere else she doesn't sing on camera.** Each of those shots becomes one of:
  - her **turned to the screen** she's presenting (profile or back), or **watching** it;
  - a **wide** where her face is too small to read a mouth, dancing with her lips together;
  - the **screen itself** full frame (slide, LED wall, laptop, phone, the call's chat) with her small in it or in a
    video tile, eyes on the content.
- **Lyrics video in those shots:** the sung line is typed big on the screen that's in the shot, word by word on the
  vocal, as part of that shot's graphic. It replaces the clay lyric row there (still one row of lyric type at a time).
  So the words are always on screen; only her mouth stops doing the work.

## What it costs and saves

- Sung clips: 45 → 16 (8 InfiniteTalk close-ups into the lens, InfiniteTalk's best case; 8 Grok phrases, 3 already
  kept).
- The other 29 shots become Grok or Wan motion without singing, 2.5D plates, or drawn screens: no lip sync to farm.
- Her share drops from 97% to roughly 75–80% (the full-frame screens), still over the 70% target.
- The film says the same things: every line is still on screen, and the lines she sings to the lens are the ones the
  film turns on (the first meeting, the ≈, the bridge's turn, "take a look", "Hi").

## Switching

Per chapter or per shot, whenever a sung shot fails its farming. The cut list doesn't change, so the edit doesn't
either; the board's route for that shot changes and the audit re-runs.

## Plan A's caveat for the stage (23 Oct night, board v2.1)

Lati: "Our own experience and reference videos have shown definitively that lipsyncing wide shot videos is hardest,
and with dancing added to it, it gets only harder. We should keep option to drop sung and lipsynced dance shots if it
gets too hard to farm grok properly and keep only dancing. earlier attempts have left me with about 38% used of the
weekly usage there. Plan b (or a's caveats) is there then to switch to full graphics mode in there, them providing the
lyrics in the style that lands words on beat. But we can try and see how far we can get."

- **Fallback for any of them:** the shot's Grok dance take with lips together (same plate, same cut), and the line
  goes on the screen in the hook voice: heavy white caps, one word in clay, each word landing on its beat
  (`HOOK_TYPE` in `build_board.py`). Wides first: a sung wide is the first to drop.
- **Order of farming, to spend Grok's week where both plans need it:**
  1. the dance takes (lips together): every stage shot needs one in plan A and plan B alike;
  2. the sung phrases already kept from chorus 1 (P1, P2, P4) are reused for the same lines in chorus 2, so chorus 2
     and the final chorus farm only what's new ("You checked me…", "You saw every line", "Nothing taken on faith");
  3. then new sung phrases, close framings (MCU, MS) first.
- **A cap per phrase:** four takes. No keeper after four, the shot takes its fallback and we move on.
- **A cap for the week:** Lati's Grok usage was ~38% on 23 Oct. Sung farming stops when it would leave too little for
  the remaining dance takes; Lati calls that point, since only he sees the meter.
